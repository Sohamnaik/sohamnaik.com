So what is metagenomics?

A spoonful of seawater or mangrove mud holds thousands of microbial species, and most can never be grown in a lab. Metagenomics reads all their DNA at once, so we can ask two questions: who is there? and what can they do?

  1. Collect

    Take a sample of water or sediment from the wild.

  2. Sequence

    Read the DNA of every organism in it.

  3. Identify

    Name the microbes and count how many of each.

  4. Understand

    Compare places, find their genes, and see what they do.

    • See why microbes drive the carbon, nitrogen and sulfur cycles
    • Learn what metagenomics can and cannot tell you, and when to use 16S or shotgun
    • Set up Galaxy and run your first analysis job

  2. 2
    Day 2

    Find the Data, Check the Reads

    Open a real sequencing project and learn to trust, or distrust, your data.

    NCBI SRAFastQCfastpMultiQC
  3. 3
    Day 3

    Who Is Out There?

    Turn millions of DNA reads into a list of named microbes.

    • Run the 16S workflow from reads to a table of microbes
    • Name them against the SILVA reference database
    • Explore the community as an interactive Krona chart and a bar chart
    DADA2SILVAKrona
  4. 4
    Day 4

    How Diverse Is It?

    Measure a community, compare places, and ask what shapes them.

    • Calculate diversity within and between samples
    • Draw an ordination map that shows how similar samples are
    • Link patterns to measured conditions, and learn why correlation is not cause
    PhyloseqAlpha & beta diversityOrdination

    Day 5

    What Are They Doing?

    Kraken2KEGGCOGCAZy

    Day 6

    Connect your results to the world's databases, then make them look good.

    • Understand how genomes are rebuilt from mixed DNA (MAGs) and how you would run it

    • Build three clear charts in Excel for your report
    GTDBUniProtExcel

    Day 7

    Show What You Found

    Present your findings and leave with a finished project.

    • Present your pod's results and check them against published science

07 Questions

Before you register.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Every analysis runs in your browser through Galaxy, a free platform used by researchers worldwide. You learn what each step does and why, which carries over to any tool you use later.

Who is this for?

Students and early-career researchers in biology, biotechnology, microbiology, marine science and related fields, who are curious about computational biology and have not done it before.

What do I need to take part?

A laptop or desktop with a stable internet connection, a free Galaxy account (we set it up together on Day 1), and Excel or Google Sheets for Day 6.

What if I miss a session?

Every live session is recorded and shared with the cohort, so you can catch up in your own time.

Will I get a certificate?

Yes, for participants who meet the completion criteria, which are shared at the start of the course.

Which batch should I choose?

Both batches teach the same course with the same instructor, so pick whichever dates suit you. Seats are limited to 25 per batch.

Ready to read the sea's DNA?

25 seats per batch. Batch 1 starts October 27, 2026.
