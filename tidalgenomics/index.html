So what is metagenomics?

A spoonful of seawater or sediment holds thousands of microbial species, and most can never be grown in a lab. Metagenomics reads all their DNA at once, so we can ask two questions: who is there? and what can they do?

  1. Collect

    Take a sample of water or sediment from the wild.

  2. Sequence

    Read the DNA of every organism in it.

  3. Identify

    Name the microbes and count how many of each.

  4. Understand

    Compare places, find their genes, and see what they do.

03 Curriculum

Seven days. One real dataset. One finished analysis.

You follow the same samples all week, so every day builds on the last.

  1. Find data
  2. Check quality
  3. Name microbes
  4. Compare
  5. Read genes
  6. Connect & graph
  7. Report
  1. 1
    Day 1

    The Invisible Ocean

    Meet the microbes that quietly run the ocean.

    • See why microbes drive the carbon, nitrogen and sulfur cycles
    • Learn what metagenomics can and cannot tell you, and when to use 16S or shotgun
    • Set up Galaxy and run your first analysis job
    Galaxy
  2. 2
    Day 2

    Find the Data, Check the Reads

    Open a real sequencing project and learn to trust, or distrust, your data.

    • Find a public project on NCBI and read its sample information
    • Understand what a sequencing file actually contains
    • Check read quality and trim the problems
    NCBI SRAFastQCfastpMultiQC
  3. 3
    Day 3

    Who Is Out There?

    Turn millions of DNA reads into a list of named microbes.

    • Run the 16S workflow from reads to a table of microbes
    • Name them against the SILVA reference database
    • Explore the community as an interactive Krona chart and a bar chart
    DADA2SILVAKrona
  4. 4
    Day 4

    How Diverse Is It?

    Measure a community, compare places, and ask what shapes them.

    • Calculate diversity within and between samples
    • Draw an ordination map that shows how similar samples are
    • Link patterns to measured conditions, and learn why correlation is not cause
    PhyloseqAlpha & beta diversityOrdination
  5. 5
    Day 5

    What Are They Doing?

    Go from who is there to what they can do.

    • Profile whole-community shotgun data
    • Read the functional annotation: pathways, gene families and enzymes
    • Compare your amplicon and shotgun results side by side
    Kraken2KEGGCOGCAZy
  6. 6
    Day 6

    Genomes, Databases and Graphs

    Connect your results to the world's databases, then make them look good.

    • Understand how genomes are rebuilt from mixed DNA (MAGs) and how you would run it
    • Follow one microbe through NCBI Taxonomy, GTDB, UniProt and the literature
    • Build three clear charts in Excel for your report
    GTDBUniProtExcel
  7. 7
    Day 7

    Show What You Found

    Present your findings and leave with a finished project.

    • Present your findings and check them against published science
    • Turn your analysis into a rerunnable Galaxy workflow
    • Submit your capstone report and get a map of what to learn next
    Galaxy workflowsCapstone

No. Every analysis runs in your browser through Galaxy, a free platform used by researchers worldwide. You learn what each step does and why, which carries over to any tool you use later.

If you are curious about how biology meets data, you belong here. The course welcomes Class 11 and 12 students exploring bioinformatics, undergraduate and postgraduate students from any life sciences background, and anyone who wants to learn bioinformatics from the ground up. No prior knowledge is needed, only curiosity and a willingness to try.

A laptop or desktop with a stable internet connection, a free Galaxy account (we set it up together on Day 1), and Excel or Google Sheets for Day 6.
